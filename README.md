# Rugby Play Designer

A free, browser-based tool for designing rugby plays: formations, player movements and phase-by-phase animation.

- **Live:** <https://playbook.samuelroiz.com>
- Built in about a week as a personal project.
- **Tech:** plain JavaScript ES modules and SVG, no framework, no build step, hosted on GitHub Pages.
- **Testing:** Node tests for the play logic, plus headless Chrome suites (desktop and phone) that drive the real app.
- **For AI assistants:** [docs/AI_PLAY_GUIDE.md](docs/AI_PLAY_GUIDE.md) explains how to write a play `.json` that loads and plays correctly, and [llms.txt](llms.txt) how to paste it in with **File → Paste play…**.

A browser whiteboard for drawing rugby plays: put attackers and defenders on a pitch, drag them around, draw runs, passes, kicks and other routes, and drop ball, ruck, scrum and lineout markers.

It is plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript (ES modules) and SVG. There is no framework and no build step, and the site needs no npm packages (they are only for the tests). A play has several phases, every play is kept in a **library** in the browser (see *Your plays*), and a play can be saved to and loaded from `.json` files. A play can start from a template. A phase, or every phase on one sheet, can be saved as a PNG, the whole play as a PDF, and the play's animation as a GIF or a video. Every change can be undone. The play can be animated phase by phase, and shown full screen in Present mode, with a pen to draw on it. A link can open the play on a given phase, and a **share link** opens it in a phone viewer where each player picks their position and watches the play follow them, with their job for each phase and short cues that pop up as a step starts. Save animation can also make that view of one position as a phone-shaped GIF or video.

## Using it

- **Add attack / Add defender**: opens a small picker of the numbers (1–15) that team isn't using yet, with the next free one highlighted. Click a number, or press Enter (or click the highlighted one) for the next free number; Escape or clicking outside closes the picker and adds nobody. Then the status line says *Click where 12 stands*, and one click on the pitch places them there (one undo step). **Escape** instead puts them at their usual spot: each team lined up across the middle of the current view, or, if another player or the direction arrow is on that spot, the nearest clear spot beside it. Anything else done meanwhile (another button, a key) puts them at the usual spot first, so a picked number is never lost. Each team can have up to 15, and the button is greyed out when a team is full. The new player is put in every phase, at the same spot, whichever phase is shown.
- **Drag** players and markers with a mouse, finger or pen. A ball is always drawn above the players, so a player holding it never hides it.
- **Double-click (or double-tap)** a player to change its label, e.g. `10` or `FH`. A small box opens just above the player: **Enter** or clicking anywhere else saves, **Escape** cancels. The app never opens a browser popup (prompt, alert or confirm): questions such as "Delete phase 2?" are asked in its own box, with OK and Cancel.
- **Welcome**: on a first visit to the site in this browser, a Welcome box (styled like About) opens after the page loads: a one-line description, the team logo and *Made for <team name>* (if set), and three buttons: **Try an example** (as in About), **Start from a template** (opens the Templates picker) and **Start blank** (just closes it). **About this project** switches to the About box. Closing it any way (a button, the link, Escape or clicking outside) records under `rugby-play-designer.welcome-seen` in `localStorage` that it was seen, so it doesn't open again in this browser; About is the way to see the information again. It doesn't open by itself on a link to a phase (`#phase=`), in Present mode, or when storage is blocked (quietly, with no message). Like About, opening and closing it doesn't change the play, undo or the autosave.
- **About** at the right end of the top bar (in the **File** menu on a phone) opens a short box about the app, with a link to the source on GitHub. It never opens by itself, and opening or closing it doesn't change the play, undo or the autosave. Escape, **Close** or clicking outside closes it, and it is hidden in Present mode. A line, *Using an AI assistant?*, has the **AI play guide** link (new tab) and **Copy guide**, which copies the guide's text and shows *Copied* on the button for 2 seconds. **Try an example** opens a finished three-phase play, *Example: Scrum 12 crash* (`templates/example.json`, which isn't in the template picker), as a new play in the library, like a template. Then the box closes and the play runs once from phase 1 at 1×.
- **Team logo** (**File → Team logo…**) opens a box to set the team's logo: **Upload image…** (PNG, JPG or SVG, up to 2 MB), an optional **team name** (up to 40 characters), a preview, **Remove logo**, and **Show logo on exports** (on by default). Each change is kept straight away; Escape, **Close** or clicking outside closes the box.
  - The logo shows about 28 px tall left of the play name, in the top left corner in Present mode, and on exports while *Show logo on exports* is on (see *Save image*, *Save PDF* and *Save animation*). If a team name is set, the About box says *Made for <team name>* above the GitHub link.
  - Pictures are shrunk to at most 512 px on their long side, keeping their shape (never enlarged). An SVG is cleaned first (scripts, event handlers, outside links and references, `foreignObject` and animation elements are removed) and then drawn to a PNG, so only PNG and JPEG pictures are ever stored.
  - It is a setting of this browser, not of the play: it's kept in `localStorage` under its own key (`rugby-play-designer.team-logo`, as a data URL), and never goes in the play, a saved `.json` file, the autosave, the undo history or the repo. Changing it is not an undo step. If it can't be stored (storage blocked or full), it is still used until the page is closed, and the box and the status line say *Couldn't save the logo setting in this browser.*
  - Until a logo is set in this browser, it uses `assets/sfv-logo.jpeg` with the team name *San Fernando Valley Rugby Club*. **Remove logo**, or uploading another, replaces that default in this browser only; the team name is a separate field. With the logo removed, the top bar, Present mode and every export look as they did without one.
- **? Tips** at the top of the sidebar opens a small popup of editing tips beside it. Clicking anywhere else or pressing Escape closes it. It floats over the page, so it never pushes the toolbar down.
- **The play's name** is at the top left. Clicking it selects the whole name, so typing replaces it. A name too long for the top bar is shortened with "…" (the tooltip has all of it), so the buttons after it never move. The name always gets at least 11rem (about 176 px), so it can be clicked and typed in at any width: when the buttons leave less than that on one row (roughly 760–1100 px wide, more with a team logo), the view controls (Full / Half, orientation, Layers) move to a second row of the top bar and About ends the first. That's decided from the buttons' widths alone (`fitTopbar` in `js/app.js`, the `.two-rows` class), so typing a long or short name never moves anything.
- **Play details**: the pencil after the name opens a small popup under it with the play's **category** (attack, defense, lineout or scrum) and **tags** (comma separated). Each change is an undo step (a run of typing is one). Escape or clicking elsewhere closes it.
- **The status line** under the pitch shows the drawing hint and short messages such as *Saved Blind side.pdf* (a message takes the hint's place for a few seconds, and a new hint, e.g. on picking a route tool, clears the message), and the route key at its right end (see *Layers → Legend*). Nothing is drawn over the field, and the line keeps its height, so the pitch never changes size when a hint appears. A hint is never cut off: one that doesn't fit on one line takes two, in smaller type, and if even that's too narrow a shorter form is shown (the full one is its tooltip). On a phone the route key has a line of its own under it.
- **Layout**: the paddings around the pitch, the top bar, the status line and the phase bar are kept small so the pitch gets as much room as possible. Under 1100 px wide the phase bar drops its longer button labels (*Prev*, *Next*, *Move*, *phase*) and narrows the note, so it takes two rows rather than three, and the top bar does the same (*Full* / *Half*, and just ⇄ for the other half, with its tooltip), so the play's name keeps room. `tests/browser/usability.mjs` measures the pitch at 1080×720 and 1500×780 with the old spacing put back in the page and with the new, and prints both sizes each run. When this was written: 829×483 → 912×532 px at 1080×720, and 1074×626 → 1107×646 px at 1500×780.
- **The sidebar** fits a 1500×780 window without scrolling, even with a player's job and cues showing (their hints are in small type, and the spacing tightens a little): the route types sit in two columns (each button its sample over its name), with tighter spacing, so Blitz and Delete are always in view.
- **Routes**: pick a route type, then press on the pitch and drag to where it ends. If you press within 2 m of a player's centre, the route starts from the nearest one: its start snaps onto them, it belongs to them and follows them when they move. That player gets a white ring while you draw (and, with a mouse, while you hover with a route tool), so you can see who it will attach to. Each type has its own colour as well as its line style: run yellow, pass white, kick orange (thick and dotted, with big dots), decoy light grey (thicker than a pass, in short dashes, so it shows on the grass), drift purple, blitz pink. Red and blue are kept for the teams. Every line has a thin dark outline so it reads on grass, white lines and the team colours.
- **Passes and kicks are drawn above the players' discs**, so the ball's path stays visible over a crowd, e.g. a lineout throw drawn as a pass from the hooker down the line. Runs, drift, blitz and decoy stay under the players. The players' numbers are above every route line (a line can cross a disc, never its number), on screen, in pictures and in the phone viewer. Badges are above everything. Pressing a player under a pass still picks up the player: only the line's look is on top.
- **A pass or kick is drawn where it happens**: from where the passer is at its step (after their runs in earlier steps and in its own), and a pass to a team-mate to where the receiver is when he catches it (see *Playing the play*), the same spots the animation uses. So a pass at step 2 after a run at step 1 starts at the end of that run. Runs, decoys, drifts and blitzes start where their player starts the phase (or, for a player's second one, where the one before ends). The pass is still stored from the passer to the receiver (`points` don't change). This is the same on the pitch, in the previous-phase ghost, in the PNG and PDF exports, in Save animation and in the phone viewer.
- **The route tool that's on** is shown near-black with a yellow edge, so it stands out from the others. As soon as one is picked, a hint in the status line under the pitch says what to do: with no player selected, *Drag from a player to draw a run, or click a player first*.
- **Click to draw from a player**: select a player, then pick a route type. A faint preview line runs from them to the pointer, and the hint says *Click where 12's run ends. Click again for another, Esc to stop* (for a pass, *… ends, or on a team-mate*; it says *defender 1* or *attacker 1* when both teams have that number). One click (or tap) on the pitch makes the route from that player. They stay selected and the tool stays on, so you can keep clicking to add more lines from them.
  - For a pass, clicking within 2 m of a team-mate ends the pass on them (they're ringed as you hover).
  - Clicking another player switches the start to them instead of ending the line there (for a pass, only an opponent does this, since a team-mate receives it). Pressing another player and dragging draws a route from them as usual, and they stay selected. A switch by a click says so in the status line, *Now drawing from 2.*, with a button to end the route on that player's spot after all, e.g. **End 1's drift on 2's spot** (a defender sliding one channel across, onto the next one's spot). It draws the route there as a click would (one undo step), keeps the first player selected, and closes the message. The message stays up for as long as the offer still applies: the same route tool on, the same phase, the second player still the start, and both players still there; turning the tool off, Escape, another phase or selecting someone else takes it away. A pass doesn't offer it (a pass doesn't end on an opponent).
  - **Esc**, clicking the route tool again, or clicking empty pitch with no tool on stops it. Each route is one undo step, and undo keeps the player selected and the tool on.
  - With no player selected, pressing and dragging draws as before. A tap (without dragging) on a player while a route tool is on selects them, which starts click-to-draw.
  - On a phone there's no hover, so the preview shows while your finger is down; the route is made where you lift it.
- **Passes attach at both ends.** If you let go of a pass within 2 m of a team-mate of the passer, its end snaps onto them and they become the receiver (`to`). The receiver is ringed while you draw, as the passer is. A pass ending near an opponent or in open grass has a loose end. When the passer or the receiver moves, the pass follows at both ends. The line stops at the edge of the receiver's disc so the arrowhead shows.
- **Editing a route**: tap or click a route to select it and show its handles. These have large invisible touch areas, so they're easy to grab with a finger.
  - Drag the **end** dot to move where the route ends. For a pass, dropping it within 2 m of a team-mate attaches it to them (ringed while you drag), and dropping it further from everyone detaches it.
  - Drag the **start** dot to move where it begins. Dropping it within 2 m of a player attaches the route to the nearest one (they're ringed while you drag). Dropping it further from everyone detaches it.
  - Drag the yellow **middle** dot to bend the route.
  - Drag the **line** to move the whole route. This only works for routes not attached to a player at either end; attached routes move with their player.

  A tap on a handle that doesn't drag goes through to whatever is under it, such as a player or a badge.
- **Route action bar**: when a route is selected (and no route tool is on), a small bar appears next to it, clear of its handles. It starts with the route's step and the routes that start with it, e.g. *Step 2, together with: pass 9 to 10* (a player's other options are alternatives, so they aren't listed). Then **Label** (options only) opens the option box with the label field ready, **Make main** (options other than A), **Order** opens the order editor (the number box, or the option box at its order field for an option), and **Delete**.
- **Route order**: each route shows a small numbered badge beside its start, placed clear of the players, balls and other badges there. A new pass or kick gets the next number (the next step). A new run, decoy, drift or blitz gets the highest number so far (1 if there's none), so it moves together with the latest step; when it shares that step with other routes, the status line says *Runs with step 2. Change the number to run before or after.* Tap or click a badge to change its number in a small box (Enter or clicking away saves, Escape cancels). Deleting a route doesn't renumber the others.
- **A second run continues the first**: a new run, decoy, drift or blitz from a player who already has one in the phase (clicked from them, or dragged from them) starts at the end of their last one and gets the next number, so they carry on running. Before it's drawn the hint says so, e.g. *Continues 8's run: click where it ends*, and afterwards the status line says *Continues 8's run as step 3.* Dragging the player moves the start of their first run; the runs that carry on stay joined to the end of the one before. They end the phase at the end of their last route (the rule the next phase is built with).
  - **Fewer badges**: a phase whose routes all share one number shows no badges (the order says nothing there). A drift or blitz shows its badge only when its number differs from every attacking route's number in that phase, since defenders usually move with the attack. *Layers → Step numbers* hides them all. The numbers are still there and still used by Play: a route whose badge is hidden can have its number changed with **Order** in the route action bar (the number box opens at the route's start). Options' badges (*2A*, *2B* …) always show, even in a phase with one step, since the letter tells them apart (unless *Step numbers* is off). **Play** runs the routes in this order (see *Playing the play*).
- **Badge hover**: with the mouse over a badge, its route gets a white halo and every other route and badge fades, until the pointer leaves the badge. On a touch screen, tapping a badge does the same while its number box is open. It's only a hint: nothing is recorded, and it's off while playing and presenting.
- **Pass and kick options**: when a player has two or more passes and/or kicks in the same phase, they are alternatives, not a sequence. Drawing a second pass or kick from a player who already has one makes both options: the one already there is **A**, the new one **B**, the next **C**, and so on. They all take the order number of the first, and their badges read e.g. *2A*, *2B*. Each option's badge sits on its own route, a short way along it from the passer and just to one side, placed so it is clearly nearer its own route than any other option of that player. Runs and the other route types are never options.
  - **A is the main plan** and draws as normal. B, C … draw a little fainter, in their usual colours.
  - Tap an option's badge to open its box. Its title names the route, e.g. *Option A: Pass to 12* (the receiver's label) or *Option B: Kick*. While it's open that route is highlighted in yellow and the other routes fade, and on a computer the box sits on the side of the pitch away from it. The box has the **order number** (shared by all of that player's options, so changing it changes them all), a short **label** of up to 25 characters (e.g. *if 13 bites*), shown just past the end of the route (as large as a player's number, on screen and in exports), and **Make this the main option**, which makes it A and re-letters the others in their old order. Each save is one undo step.
  - Deleting an option closes the letters up (A, C → A, B). When only one is left it's a normal route again: its letter goes, and its label is no longer shown. Deleting a player removes passes to them, which re-letters the passer's options the same way. Moving a route's start onto another player takes it out of the first player's options and adds it to the new player's as the last letter.
  - **Add phase** and **Insert after** follow option A only (see below). Every export (PNG, all-phases PNG, PDF) shows options and labels as the pitch does. The previous-phase ghost shows only option A of each player, the plan the next phase was built from.
  - Older plays, without option letters, load and work as before. A player in an old play who already has two passes only gets letters when another pass or kick is drawn from them.
- **Set piece**: after choosing Scrum or Lineout (and who has the put-in or throw), the status line says *Click where the mark goes*, and the next click on the pitch places it there (one undo step), snapped as below. **Escape** (or anything else done meanwhile) cancels it. Each set piece always reuses the phase's ball nearest the mark, moving it into place, and only adds one if the phase has none.
- **Set piece → Scrum**: puts players 1–8 of both teams in a scrum formation: front row 1-2-3, locks 4-5, flankers 6-7, 8 at the back, each loosehead facing the opposing tighthead. Both 9s go on the put-in side: the put-in team's 9 next to the ball, the other 9 by their own pack. Players 1–9 that already exist are moved rather than duplicated, so a team never goes over 15. The 9s are part of the scrum and move with it. The ball is placed on the put-in side as an ordinary ball you can select, move or delete. The **S** block only shows while the scrum or one of its players is selected. Drag it to move the whole scrum, along with a ball at the scrum. The mark (where you click, or where you drag it) snaps across the pitch to the nearest of the left 15 m line, the middle or the right 15 m line; along the pitch it can go anywhere in the field of play. Players can still be dragged one at a time afterwards. Running the preset again moves the same players to the new scrum.
- **Set piece → Lineout**: forms the lineout where you click, using the phase's lineout marker (one is added if there isn't one). The marker snaps to the 5 m line of the touchline nearer the click, and its position along the pitch is the line of touch. It's the same formation as the Lineout template: 1, 4, 3, 6, 5, 8 and 7 of each team in two lines from 5 m in (attack on the side nearer their own try line), the throwing team's hooker on the touchline, the other hooker in the 5 m channel, and both 9s behind the lineout. The ball nearest the marker moves to the thrower (one is added if there's none). As with the scrum, players that exist are moved, not duplicated. It works at either touchline.

  The lineout then moves as a group, like the scrum. While the marker or one of its players is selected, an **L** block shows beside the defense's line (its tooltip and label say *Drag to move the lineout*; the scrum's **S** says *Drag to move the scrum*). Drag it, or the marker itself (its half nearer touch), to move every player the preset placed, along with the ball at the thrower. It moves along the touchline only: it stays on the 5 m line of its own touchline, and far enough in for both lines to stay in the field of play. To set up at the other touchline, run the preset again and click by that touchline. Players can still be dragged one at a time afterwards and keep their offset. Running the scrum preset takes its players out of the lineout's group (and the other way round), so no player belongs to both. A lineout marker added on its own, without the preset, drags freely and moves nobody.
- **Select** by clicking a player, route or marker. **Delete** (key or button) removes it. **Esc** clears the selection and the route tool.
- **Full / Half** switches the view. Half shows one half, touchline to touchline, and 5 m past halfway (`HALF_MARGIN` in `js/pitch.js`), so players on or near halfway (at a kick-off, say, including the chasers a few metres behind it) are whole on the pitch rather than off its edge; the pitch is sized to fill the space without showing any extra field. Switching to Half shows the half where most of the shown phase's players are: halfway (x = 50) to the dead ball line (x = 110), the half attack plays into, shown from x = 45, or the dead ball line at x = −10 to halfway, shown to x = 55 (on a tie, the first). While in a half view, **⇄ Other half** beside Full / Half flips to the other one; in the full view it's hidden but keeps its place in the top bar, so the buttons beside it never shift. The choice is kept in the play's `view` (`"half"` or `"half-own"`), so it's saved and undoable like other view changes. A player off the shown view gets a small arrow at its edge, in their team's colour with their number, pointing the way to them.
- **Horizontal / Vertical** turns the pitch. Vertical puts the try lines at the top and bottom, with x = 110 at the top. Only the view turns: the data stays the same, and numbers, labels and badges stay upright. It starts vertical on a phone held upright and horizontal otherwise, and follows the phone as it turns until you pick one yourself. Turning the pitch, by hand or by itself, is not an undo step: it doesn't clear Redo, and Undo never turns the pitch back.
- **Layers**:
  - *Distances* labels the lines like a real pitch (5m, 22, 10, 50). Halfway is labelled "50" only, on the side of the half shown in a half view, and the 10 m labels sit further along their lines (20 m in from touch), so the three don't run together.
  - *Grid* adds a faint 5 m grid.
  - *Legend* shows or hides the route key, at the right end of the status line under the pitch, never over it, in every view and orientation (never shown in Present mode).
  - *Player size*: **Small**, **Medium** (the default) or **Large** scales player discs, their numbers and the route badges (0.8×, 1×, 1.2×). It's a setting of this browser, kept in `localStorage` (`rugby-play-designer.player-size`), not in the play, its files, the autosave or the undo history. Saved images and PDFs use the size on screen.
  - *Previous phase* draws the last phase's players as faint dashed outlines with their numbers, and its routes as faint lines in their usual colours, so you can see who moved where. None of it can be clicked, and the ghost routes have no order badges or handles, so they can't be mistaken for the current phase's. Saved images and PDFs never include it.

  - *Step numbers* shows or hides the route order badges (see *Route order*), on screen and in every export (PNG, all-phases PNG, PDF). It's a setting of this browser, kept in `localStorage` (`rugby-play-designer.step-numbers`), not in the play, its files, the autosave or the undo history.
  - *Direction* shows **Attack** and an arrow in the attack's red near the bottom edge of the pitch, pointing the way attack plays (towards x = 110): right on a horizontal pitch, up on a vertical one. If a player in the shown phase is on that spot, it moves along the same edge to the nearest place clear of every player. It's included in saved images and PDFs.

  Everything except Grid starts on. Player size and Step numbers are remembered in this browser; the others reset when the page is opened.

### Templates

A new play (first visit, or **File → New play**) starts as an empty pitch with no players. **Templates** in the top bar opens a picker with a card per template: a small picture of its pitch, drawn from the template's own data, with its name under it. Each card's picture (like each row's in Plays) is zoomed to fit the players and markers in phase 1, with about 8 m round them, in the full pitch's shape and never smaller than a quarter of the pitch (60 × 35 m); the player discs are drawn larger than on the pitch so they read at that size, and a play with no players shows the full pitch. Picking one opens it as a new play in the library, so the play you had stays in Plays and nothing is asked. The template's view (full or half) is used, but it takes the orientation you have on now.

The starter templates (attack plays towards x = 110, as always):

- **Blank**: an empty pitch.
- **Backline attack**: all 15 attackers. A ruck 20 m in from the left touchline with 9 at it, the forwards in a pod off the ruck (two guarding the short side), and the backs in a flat-ish line out to 14 on the open side, with 11 on the short side and 15 deep.
- **Attack vs defense**: the same attack, and the defense in a flat line 3 m beyond the ruck, with their 9 behind it and 15 sweeping.
- **Scrum**: both packs and 9s from the scrum preset on the middle spot (attack's put-in), and both backlines 5 m behind their No. 8, on the put-in side, so each 10 stands on the same side of the scrum as their 9 and the 9 to 10 pass never crosses a pack.
- **Lineout**: the lineout preset (see *Set piece → Lineout*) at a marker 5 m in from touch on the attack's 10 m line, with the attack throwing in, and both backlines 10 m back, further infield than the 9s, so 9 to 10 is clear of the lineout.
- **Kick-off**: 10 kicking off from the centre spot with the attack spread behind halfway, and the receiving team spread in their half, 10 m or more back.

Players are discs 2.4 m across, which is wider than a real body, so tight formations (the scrum, the lineout) are spaced to stop the discs overlapping, as in the scrum preset.

#### Making your own template

Templates are ordinary play files in `templates/`, listed in `templates/index.json`:

```json
[
  { "name": "Blank", "file": "blank.json" },
  { "name": "Backline attack", "file": "backline-attack.json" }
]
```

To turn a play into a template, save it from the app (**File → Save file**), copy the `.json` into `templates/` and add a line for it to `index.json`. `name` is what the card shows, and the cards are in the order of the list. The play's own name, category, tags, view, phases, routes and markers all come with it. Templates are fetched from the site, so they work on GitHub Pages and on a local server, but not when `index.html` is opened as a file.

### Phases

The bar under the pitch shows the phase tabs (**1 2 3 … +**), **Prev** and **Next**, the play controls (see *Playing the play*), and "Phase 2 of 4". The **←** and **→** keys also go back and forward.

- **+** (after the tabs) adds a phase at the end, built from the last phase, whichever phase is shown.
- **Insert after** adds a phase straight after the current one, built from it. The phases after it are kept exactly as they were (positions, routes, notes, markers); only their numbers move up by one.
- A new phase is built the same way either way:
  - Each player with a run, drift, blitz or decoy starting on them moves to the end of that route. If a player has several, the highest order number wins.
  - The ball follows the passes and kicks and ends with the highest-order one. Options other than A (B, C …) are left out, so with options the ball follows A. If the phase has more than one ball, the one that moves is the ball nearest the start of the first pass or kick.
    - For a pass, the ball moves to the receiver, where they stand after their own run. The receiver is the pass's `to` player. If the pass has none, it's the nearest team-mate of the passer within 5 m of where the pass lands, with everyone where they stand at that point (after their runs up to the pass's order); if nobody is that close, the ball goes to the end of the pass.
    - For a kick, the ball goes to where the kick ends.
    - With no passes or kicks the ball stays where it is.
  - Routes aren't copied, so the new phase starts with none. Markers (ruck, scrum, lineout, ball) are copied.
  - The animation uses the same rules (they live in `js/model.js`), so it always moves the same ball to the same receiver as the next phase was built with.
- **◂ Move / Move ▸** swaps the current phase with its neighbour. It never changes what is in any phase. **Delete phase** asks first.
- Adding, inserting, moving and deleting a phase are each one undo step.
- **A player's job and cues**: with one player selected, the side panel (the toolbar; on a phone, its right end) shows these, and scrolls by itself (the panel, never the page) just enough to bring all of them into view when a different player is selected. Scrolling it by hand afterwards sticks until another player is picked.
  - **Your job**, with the hint *One line shown at the bottom of the phone viewer.* under it: a box for that player's job in this phase (its label for screen readers names them: *10's job in this phase*), one short line of up to 120 characters (e.g. *Flat, call for it early.*). It's saved in the phase as `instructions` (see *Coordinates and data*), autosaved and undoable (one undo step for a run of typing). Left empty, the phone viewer and a position's animation show a job made from the player's routes instead, and the box's placeholder shows it, e.g. *12: Run your line, then take the pass from 10* (see *Auto jobs*). The made one is never saved.
  - **Cues**, with the hint *Short shouts that pop up at a moment.* under it: short calls that pop up next to the player in the phone viewer (and in Present mode with *I play*) as a step starts, e.g. *JUMP!* at step 2. (On a phone, where the side panel is a row at the bottom, the hints aren't shown: the box's placeholder says whose job it is.) Each is listed in capitals with its step, **Edit** and **Delete**. **Add cue** (or Edit) opens a small form: the text (up to 30 characters, shown in capitals), **at step** (a list of the order numbers of this phase's routes, starting on the player's first route's step, or 1), **Save** (or Enter) and **Cancel** (or Escape). Several per player per phase are fine. Saving and deleting are each one undo step, and autosaved. They're kept in the phase as `cues`.
  - While the player is selected, their cues for the phase show as small yellow bubbles stacked over them on the pitch (*2 JUMP!*: step and text). They're an editing aid: never in Present mode, while playing, or in a PNG, PDF or the whole team's animation.

  Each phase has its own; a new phase built with **+** or **Insert after** starts with none, and deleting a player removes theirs.
- **Auto jobs** (`js/jobs.js`): a player with no written job gets one made from their routes in the phase (option A, as it plays): *Pass to 12*; *Take the pass from 10* when they receive one; a kick by its length and direction (forward for their team, measured from where it's taken): *Grubber* (under 15 m), *Box kick* (15 to 35 m, only when the kicker is 9 or kicks from within 5 m of a ruck in the phase; anyone else's kick of that length is *Kick*), *Long kick* (35 m or more), *Cross-field kick* (20 m or more across, and more across than forward), else *Kick*; *Chase the kick* for a run that ends within 10 m of where a kick in the phase lands; *Cover the backfield* for a wing or the fullback (11, 14 or 15) whose run goes back more than 1 m, or who stays 5 m or more behind where a team-mate's kick is taken while other team-mates chase it (with no run, or a run that doesn't chase); *Carry into contact* for a ball carrier whose last run ends within 4 m of a ruck that's new in the next phase; *Run your line*, *Decoy run*, *Drift across*, *Blitz up*; and *Hold your spot* when they do nothing. Two of them are joined in the order they happen (*Take the pass from 10, then carry into contact*); when there are more, the ones with the ball come first.
- The **note** box beside them belongs to the current phase. It keeps a usable width however many phases there are (the phase tabs scroll sideways instead), and a fixed height, as tall as the buttons beside it, showing two lines: a longer note wraps and scrolls inside it, so typing a note never makes the pitch jump. When the tabs, play controls and phase tools don't fit on one row, the tools and note move to a second row; on narrower screens the note gets a row of its own. In Present mode it is the caption under the pitch.
- Players belong to the whole play: deleting a player removes them from every phase, along with their routes and any passes to them, and takes them out of any scrum or lineout group.

### Playing the play

**Play** in the phase bar animates the play from the phase shown through to the last one.

- Within a phase, the routes run in badge order. Routes with the same order number start together; order 2 starts once everything in order 1 has finished.
- A player with a run, drift, blitz or decoy moves along the route's curve to its end. A player with two such routes does them one after the other.
- The ball travels along each pass or kick, starting once the passer has finished their own movement in that order step (so a player who runs and passes in step 1 runs first, then passes), from where the passer is then. Before that the ball is carried: a ball within 3 m of the passer at the start of the phase moves with them. A pass to a team-mate (`to`) ends in their hands. **Catch on the run**: if they're still running at the pass's step (a pop pass), the ball goes to the point on their run where they are when it gets there, worked out along their curved run with the ball at pass speed, rather than chasing them, and they carry on with it; the pass's arrow ends at that catch point. If they've finished their run, it goes to where they finish. A flight takes as long as a pass (or kick) at its pace over the distance it actually covers. If a phase has a pass but no ball marker, a ball is drawn for the animation only.
- Only option **A** plays. B, C … stay faint and never move (in Present mode another option can be played, see below).
- A route's order badge disappears as soon as that route starts moving, and comes back when the play stops or another phase is shown.
- Then a short slide moves everyone, including players with no route, and the markers (ball, ruck …) to exactly where they are in the next phase, and that phase's routes play. Markers that aren't in the next phase fade out on the way.
- **Play / Pause**: the button (*▶ Play* / *⏸ Pause*, the icon first with the same gap before the label, in the phase bar and in Present mode) or the **space bar**. Pausing freezes the exact frame on screen, with Loop on or off, mid-route or mid-slide, and Play carries on from that moment. The frame gives way to the phase as it really is (so editing always uses real positions) when the play is changed, another phase is shown, or an edit starts: a press on the pitch, a new selection, a route tool picked, or a text box focused. After the last phase it stops on the last phase's final frame, and (with Loop off) the status line says **End of play** with a **Replay** button, until the play is edited, another phase is shown, or Play is pressed. Replay, or Play, starts again from phase 1.
- **Loop**: after the last phase, the play holds it for 1.5 seconds, then everyone slides back to their places in phase 1 (no jump) and it plays again. Turning Loop on or off while the last phase is showing keeps the frame where it is.
- **Step back / forward one phase**: **Prev** / **Next**, or the **←** / **→** keys (in Present mode, ‹ and ›; ⏮ goes back to phase 1). While playing, it carries on playing from the start of that phase; while paused, it just shows it. Clicking a phase tab works the same way. None of these (nor a `#phase` link) change the phase in the middle of a drag.
- **Speed**: 0.5×, 1× or 2×. 1× is an easy pace to follow on a TV (what used to be 0.5×): a run goes at about 3.5 m/s, a pass at 9 m/s and a kick at 11 m/s, within limits so that very short and very long routes stay watchable. Changing the speed while paused only sets it: the frame stays, and Play carries on from that moment at the new speed (the same in Present mode and the phone viewer).
- While playing, editing is locked: nothing on the pitch can be dragged or drawn, and the tools, undo and redo (buttons and keys), Templates and the phase editing buttons are greyed out. The File menu can't be used with the mouse or the keyboard (it's inert, so Tab skips it). The previous-phase ghost is hidden. Pause (or the end of the play) returns to normal editing on the phase it stopped at. **Esc** pauses.
- **Typing is never lost**: focusing a text box while the play is playing or paused (the phase note, the play's name, or Play details' category and tags, by a click, a tap or Tab) stops the play and drops the paused frame, so the box can be typed in straight away and what's typed is kept. While a picture or animation is being made, these boxes are disabled (greyed out) instead.
- Each animation frame only moves the players and markers whose place has changed (it uses `requestAnimationFrame`).

### Present mode

**Present** in the top bar shows the pitch alone, for a TV or a screen recording: full screen (when the browser allows it), with the toolbar, top bar, status line, phase bar and route key hidden, and the pitch as large as fits. The phase's note is a large caption under the pitch, always shown in full (the pitch gets smaller for a long note), and *Phase n of N* sits in the top right corner. The team logo, if there is one, sits small in the top left corner, clear of the caption and the controls.

- The play controls (⏮ to phase 1, ‹ previous phase, Play/Pause, › next phase, speed, Loop) sit between the pitch and the caption, with **I play**, **Pen**, the pen colours, **Undo stroke**, **Clear** and **Exit**, so they never cover the pitch or the note. They fade out (and so does the mouse pointer) after 2 seconds without mouse movement, and come back when the mouse moves. They're always on one row, inside the window: when the window is too narrow for all of them (below about 1150 px), the least used move into **More ▴** at the bar's right, before Exit, in this order until the rest fit: the pen colours, Undo stroke, Clear, Loop, the speed, I play, Pen, ⏮, the option buttons, ‹ and ›. Play, More and Exit always stay on the bar. More opens a panel upwards with them in bar order; a button there does its job and closes it. Resizing the window refits the bar, and leaving Present mode puts every control back in its place. The keys work as usual: **Space**, **←** / **→**.
- **Option A / B / C**: when the phase shown has pass or kick options, a button for each appears. **Option B** plays that phase with B instead of A, so the ball follows B (B draws as the main option meanwhile), and stops at the end of the phase, since the next phase was built from A. **Option A**, the default, plays on as usual.
- **I play**: click a player on the pitch (or pick one from the list: **None**, then both teams by number, our team first, e.g. *Defense 13* … *Attack 13* for a defense play) and the rest of their team, with their routes, fades; the opposition stays at full strength. The chosen player gets a yellow ring. Clicking them again (or **None** in the list) clears it. It stays on through Play and phase changes, and goes when you leave Present mode. As in the phone viewer:
  - **Their job line** takes the phase note's place under the pitch: their number in a yellow tag and their job for the phase (written, or made from their routes; see *Auto jobs*), in large bold type. The caption always keeps room for one line (as a one-line note does), or the note's height if that's more, and the job line is shrunk to fit that room, on at most two lines, so choosing *I play*, or None, never moves the controls or the pitch.
  - **Their cues** pop up in a bold yellow bubble next to them as each one's step starts, beside them and inside the pitch, never over their disc, and moving with them; each stays up 1.5 s (paused, it stays). Unlike the viewer, playback doesn't slow down for them, so the coach's timing is kept.
- **Pen**: **P** (or the Pen button) turns on a freehand pen over the pitch, thick enough to see on a TV. Three colours: black with a white outline (the default), cyan (`#00e5ff`) with a dark outline, and magenta with a white outline; picking one also turns the pen on. None of them is a route's or a team's colour. Picking up the pen pauses the play, so you draw on a still picture. **Undo stroke** (or **Z** while the pen is on) takes back the last stroke. The marks stay, through phase changes, until **C** (or Clear); they aren't saved and go when you leave, and the pen goes back to black.
- Nothing can be edited while presenting: there are no hover hints, handles, selection highlights or tooltips, the ghost is hidden, and undo and redo (buttons and keys) do nothing.
- **Escape** (or Exit) leaves Present mode. Leaving stops the play and drops the paused frame, so editing is unlocked straight away on the phase shown. In full screen the browser's own Escape leaves full screen, and Present mode with it.

### Links to a phase

The address shows the phase being shown, e.g. `…/#phase=3`, and follows it as phases change (including while playing). Opening an address with `#phase=n` starts on that phase (a number past the end opens the last one). The play itself is the one this tab (or, in a new tab, the last one) had open, so the link is for coming back to a phase, or for sharing one together with the play's `.json` file.

### Share link and the phone viewer

**File → Share link…** makes a link that opens the play on any phone, read-only, with nothing to install and no account: `https://playbook.samuelroiz.com/view.html#p=<data>`. `<data>` is the play as JSON, compressed with `CompressionStream("deflate-raw")` and base64url-encoded (`js/share.js`). It's all in the part after `#`, which browsers never send to the server, so the play isn't stored anywhere: whoever has the link has the play.

- The box (`#share-dialog`) makes the link and shows it in a read-only field with its length (*Link ready (N characters)*). Nothing is copied until **Copy** is pressed (*Link copied (N characters)*; if the clipboard can't be used, the link is selected in the box to copy by hand). **Share…** opens the device's share sheet with the link (`navigator.share`), and is hidden in browsers that have none. **Open** opens the link in a new tab.
- **Include team logo** (on by default) puts the team name and logo (*Team logo…*) in the link; off leaves both out. The club's default logo costs nothing (the viewer has it); an uploaded one makes the link much longer.
- A link over 16,000 characters gets a warning that some apps may cut it (and then it won't open), with **Drop the logo** when the logo is in it.
- Served from `localhost` or `127.0.0.1` (running it locally, and the tests), the link points to that server's `view.html` instead, so it can be tried before deploying.

The viewer (`view.html`, `view.css`, `js/view.js`) is made for a phone held upright. It loads only the pitch drawing, the animation and the play checks (no editor, library, autosave or Welcome box), and changes nothing in the browser except remembering the last position picked.

- **Our team**: the play's category decides whose view players get. A *defense* play is the defense's: its defenders are listed and followed first. Any other category (attack, lineout, scrum) is the attack's. One helper, `ourTeam` in `js/model.js`, decides it for the viewer, Present mode's *I play* and Save animation's *Who*.
- **Start**: the play name, the team logo and name (if in the link), *Which position do you play?* with a large button for each of our team's numbers, and **Watch the whole play**. When both teams have players, a small **Attack / Defense** switch above the numbers (on our team to start with) lists the other side instead; back on this screen with ☰, it shows the followed player's team. The last position picked in this browser is preselected only on its own team: it's kept as its team and number (`rugby-play-designer.viewer-position` in localStorage, e.g. `{"team":"defense","number":"10"}`; an older plain `10` is the attack's 10), so the attack's 10 never preselects the defense's 10.
- **Following a position**: the pitch is vertical, attack playing up, and the view glides after that player, zoomed so the 20 m round them stays in view, and the ball too when it's within 25 m of them. The player has a yellow ring; their team-mates fade, and opponents within 15 m of them stay at full strength (the others fade less). Following a defender works the same way, with their job line (written, or made: *Drift across*, *Blitz up*, *Hold your spot* …) and their cues. The pitch keeps the editor's direction (attack playing up) for either team; the header says *Phase n of N* and whose view it is, *Attack 10* or *Defense 13*. It starts playing straight away at 1×.
- **Your job**: the bottom shows *YOUR JOB* and the player's job for the phase: their written instruction, or else the one made from their routes (see *Auto jobs*), in large bold text, made smaller if needed to fit at most two lines. The bottom keeps its height, so the pitch never changes size.
- **Coach's note**: a small button beside *YOUR JOB* (shown when the phase has a note) opens a sheet from the bottom with the job and the phase's whole note. Tapping the job line opens it too. Playing pauses while it's open and carries on when it's closed (**Close**, Escape or a tap outside).
- **Cues**: when a cue's step starts (when the first route with that order number starts moving; a step with no routes, the next step that has some), a bold yellow bubble with its text in capitals pops up next to the player: above them, or below or beside them near an edge, always inside the view and never over their disc. It stays up for 1.5 s, and meanwhile playback eases to half speed for about a second, so it registers. Several cues at once stack in one bubble. Only the followed player's cues show, and none in the replay of the whole play.
- **Switching position**: ☰ at any moment (whole play, following a player, or the replay) goes back to the positions, and picking one then follows that player from the start of the phase that was showing: their job line and cues from there on, none of the whole-play notes, the view gliding to them. *Watch the whole play* there carries on from that phase too. After the end (*Another position*), or on a fresh link, it starts at phase 1.
- **Controls**, all at least 44 px: ☰ (back to the positions), ‹ Back and › Next (a phase at a time; also a swipe right or left on the pitch, or ← and →), Play/Pause (also the space bar), and 0.5× / 1×.
- **The end**: after the last phase it zooms out to the whole play and replays it once with everyone at full strength and the phase notes as captions, the header still saying whose view it was, *Attack 10 · replay* (› on the last phase goes straight to this replay) (each in full: a long note scrolls, fades at the bottom to show there's more, and a tap on it opens it out to show all of it, a second tap folds it back; a note is never cut off), then shows the play name with **Watch again**, **Another position** and **Share** (the phone's share sheet with the same link; where there is none, it copies the link).
- **Watch the whole play** goes straight to that whole-play view, then the end.
- A broken or cut link (or none) shows a short message and a link to the designer.
- On iPhone: no sideways scroll, the safe areas (notch, home bar) are kept clear (`viewport-fit=cover` and `env(safe-area-inset-*)`), and double-tapping doesn't zoom the page (`touch-action: manipulation`). Each frame only moves what changed, with `requestAnimationFrame`.

### Your plays (the library)

Every play you make is kept in this browser, in a library of plays. **Your plays live only in this browser**, on this device: they aren't sent anywhere, there's no account, and clearing the browser's site data (or using another browser or device) means they aren't there. **Plays → Back up all plays** is how to keep them safe, or to move them to another device: it downloads one file with all of them, which **Restore from backup…** reads back in.

- **Storage**: plays are stored in IndexedDB, one record per play (an id, the play, the phase last shown, when it was created and last edited, and when it was deleted, if it's in Recently deleted). Where IndexedDB isn't available, localStorage is used instead (one key per play, `rugby-play-designer.play.<id>`). If neither can be used (storage blocked), the plays last until the page is closed, and the status line says *Plays can't be saved in this browser (storage is blocked). Use Plays → Back up all plays to keep your work.*
- **The open play is always one of the library's plays.** It's saved to its entry shortly after every change, and straight away when the page is hidden or closed. If a save fails because storage is full, the play stays on screen and in the library for this visit, and the status line says *Couldn't save: this browser's storage is full. Your play is still here: use Plays → Back up all plays to keep it.* Each tab remembers which play it has open, so a reload brings that one back; a new tab opens the last play opened in any tab.
- **First run**: a browser that used the designer before the library had one autosaved play (`rugby-play-designer.autosave` in localStorage). It's moved into the library as a play and opened, so nobody loses their current play; the old key is removed once it's safely stored. If it can't be read, it's kept under a backup key (`rugby-play-designer.autosave-backup`), a blank play opens, and the status line says *Your last play couldn't be opened. A backup was kept.* (The same message shows if a play can't be drawn when the page loads: it stays in the library and a blank play opens.)
- **Nothing is ever replaced by accident**: **File → New play**, choosing a template, **Try an example** and **Load file…** each make a new play in the library and open it, without asking, and the play you had stays in Plays as it was. (If the play you had is a completely empty *Untitled play*, it isn't kept, so the list doesn't fill up with empty plays.)
- **Plays** in the top bar (in the **File** menu on a phone) opens the list of every play, most recently edited first. Each row has a small picture of phase 1 (zoomed to its players, like the template cards), the name, the category, the tags, how many phases and when it was last edited; the open play is marked *Open now*. A search box (names and tags) and a category filter narrow it. Each row has:
  - **Open** (or click the row): opens it, with a fresh undo history (the play you leave is saved first).
  - **Duplicate**: adds *<name> copy* to the list.
  - **Rename**: the name becomes a field in the row; Enter or clicking away saves, Escape cancels. Renaming the open play is an ordinary edit (one undo step).
  - **Delete**: moves it to **Recently deleted**, at the bottom of the list (collapsed), with **Restore** and **Delete forever** (which asks first). The last 20 deleted plays are kept; older ones go for good. Deleting the open play opens the most recently edited other one, or, if there is none, one new blank *Untitled play*. A blank, empty *Untitled play* is removed for good rather than moved to Recently deleted, so deleting the open play again and again never fills Recently deleted with blank plays.
- **Back up all plays** downloads `rugby-plays-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json`, with every play in the library, Recently deleted ones marked as such. **Restore from backup…** reads such a file, checks every play like Load file does, and **adds** them to the library; it never changes a play that's there. A play whose id is already here is skipped if it's the same. If it differs, the update times decide: when the library's copy was edited as recently as the backup's or more recently, it's kept as it is and nothing is added (*kept newer*); when the backup's is newer, or either has no update time to compare, the backup's is added beside it with a new id and *(restored)* after its name. It then says what happened, e.g. *Added 12 plays, 2 skipped (already here), 1 kept newer (the copy here is newer)* (and how many couldn't be read, if any).
- **Two tabs**: they share the library, and each tab's Plays list follows changes made in the other. Tabs with different plays open never get in each other's way. If another tab changes the play open in this one, this tab asks *This play was changed in another tab*: **Use theirs** loads it (one undo step), **Keep mine** saves this one again. Nothing is saved here while the question is open. Another tab only moving between phases or turning the pitch doesn't count as a change.

### Undo and files

- **Undo / Redo** (top bar, or Ctrl/Cmd+Z and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z) covers every change to the open play, including phases, labels, order numbers, name, note and details. Opening another play (from Plays, New play, a template, the example or Load file) starts a fresh undo history. A whole drag is one step, and so is a burst of typing in one box. Up to 200 steps are kept, fewer if the history would pass about 20 MB (the oldest go first). Turning the pitch isn't a step (see *Horizontal / Vertical*), and nor is anything that only shows a hint (hovering, the click-to-draw preview, rings). Undo and redo do nothing while playing, presenting or making a picture. History is cleared on refresh.
- The **File** menu (Escape closes it; in a short window it scrolls inside itself, and the page behind never scrolls) holds:
  - **Plays…** (on a phone): the Plays list (see *Your plays*).
  - **New play**: opens a new blank play (the one you had stays in Plays).
  - **Save file**: downloads the play as `<play name>.json`. The name is cleaned of characters that aren't allowed in file names, and the JSON is pretty-printed.
  - **Load file…**: reads a `.json` play. It checks the file has `schemaVersion`, `players` and `phases`, and shows a short error if not. Every other field is checked too, and one of the wrong type is dropped or given its default, so a damaged or hand-edited file can't break the app (see *Coordinates and data*). It opens as a new play in the library (the one you had stays in Plays), and the status line says so.
  - **Paste play…**: opens a box (`#paste-dialog`) with a large text box labelled *Play JSON* (`#paste-play-text`), a choice of **Add as a new play** (the default each time the box opens) or **Replace the open play** (`name="paste-mode"`, values `new` and `replace`), **Import** (`#paste-play-import`) and **Cancel**. Paste a play's JSON, e.g. one an AI assistant wrote from the AI play guide, and press **Import** (or Ctrl/Cmd+Enter). It's checked with the same rules as Load file. A usable play opens as a new play in the library (the one you had stays in Plays), or with *Replace the open play* it takes the open play's place under the same library entry, as one undo step, and the version it replaced goes to **Recently deleted** as its own play, so it can be restored (a blank, empty *Untitled play* isn't kept). Either way the box closes and the status line says *Imported <name>*. Otherwise the box stays open with the text and says why, e.g. *That isn't a play: it isn't valid JSON.* or *That text isn't a rugby play: it has no phases.* Cancel or Escape closes it and changes nothing.
  - **AI play guide** opens [docs/AI_PLAY_GUIDE.md](docs/AI_PLAY_GUIDE.md) in a new tab, and **Copy guide** copies its text to the clipboard (the status line says *Copied the AI play guide*).
  - **Save image (PNG)…**: asks *This phase* or *All phases*.

    *This phase* is the phase being shown, at twice its size on screen, in the current view and orientation, with the layers that are on (distances, grid, direction). If the Legend layer is on, the route key goes in a white strip under the pitch, never on it. With a team logo and *Show logo on exports* on, the logo sits in the top right corner, about 8% of the picture's width, in a white strip above the pitch (never on it). The previous-phase ghost, selection highlights and handles are always left out. It is named `<play name>-phase-<n>.png`. On a phone or tablet it opens the share sheet instead of downloading (on iPhone, *Save Image* puts it in Photos). If the share sheet can't open, the picture is shown in a box: press and hold it to save, or use *Open in new tab* or *Share…*.

    *All phases* is one PNG with the play name at the top (and the team logo, if it's shown on exports, in the top right corner at about 8% of the width, with the title row made taller so it never reaches a pitch) and every phase in a grid: one pitch per row for a horizontal play and 2 per row for a vertical one (or 1, for a single phase), so the pitches, players and badges are drawn large. Each tile is "Phase n" over the pitch, with the phase's note under it, cut to two lines with "…". The route key goes once under the grid (never on a pitch), if the Legend layer is on. Like the other pictures, it has no ghost, highlights or handles. It is named `<play name>-all-phases.png`, and goes to the share sheet on a phone or tablet, with the same fallback box. A play with a great many phases is drawn smaller so the picture stays under the size phones can make.
  - **Save PDF (all phases)**: downloads `<play name>.pdf` straight away (no print dialog), with one A4 page per phase. Each page has a header line with the play name on the left (with the team logo just left of it, if it's shown on exports) and its category and tags (e.g. *Attack · backs, set move*) on the right. Then comes the pitch picture, as large as fits: the full page width on a horizontal play, and the height left on a vertical one. It's made the same way as the PNG, so it has no ghost or handles, but its lines and distance labels are dark so they survive black and white printing (the screen keeps white lines). Under the pitch, "Phase n of N" is on the left and the route key on the right (if the Legend layer is on), then the phase's note. Horizontal plays get landscape pages and vertical ones portrait. A very long note is cut short with "…". The PDF's built-in font only covers Western European letters, so a name or note with anything else (macrons such as *ā*, other alphabets, emoji) is drawn as a picture of the text instead. On a phone or tablet it goes to the share sheet like the PNG (*Save to Files* on iPhone); if that can't open, a box offers *Open in new tab* and *Share…*.

    The PDF is made with [jsPDF](https://github.com/parallax/jsPDF) **4.2.1** (MIT licence), kept in the repo as `vendor/jspdf.umd.min.js` with its licence in `vendor/jspdf.LICENSE`. It isn't fetched from a CDN, and it is only loaded the first time you save a PDF, so ordinary page loads don't download it. To update it, replace both files from the `dist/jspdf.umd.min.js` and `LICENSE` of the new npm package and change the version here.

  - **Save animation…**: saves the play as it plays (every phase, option A, the same timing as **Play**), opening a box (`#animation-dialog`) with:
    - **Who**: *Whole team* (the default, as described here), then each player of both teams by number, our team first (the defense for a defense play), named with their team: *Attack 10*, *Defense 13* … (see *A position's animation* below).
    - **Format**: *GIF: plays everywhere, loops* (the default) or *Video: best quality (MP4, or WebM if the browser can't make MP4)*. A video plays once: it doesn't loop by itself, and the box says so. Video is greyed out in a browser that can't record a canvas.
    - **Speed**: 0.5×, 1× (the default) or 2×, as in the phase bar.
    - **Size**: *Small* (640 px wide), *Medium* (960, the default) or *Large* (1280). The height follows the pitch's shape (a vertical play is taller than it is wide).

    **Save** draws the animation frame by frame. A progress bar shows how far it has got, and **Cancel** (or Escape) stops it and saves nothing (*Animation cancelled*). Editing is locked meanwhile, as while playing. Then it downloads `<play name>.gif`, `.mp4` or `.webm` and says *Saved <file>* (on a phone or tablet, the share sheet, as for the PNG).

    Each frame has a header (the team logo, if *Show logo on exports* is on, the play name in bold and *Phase n of N*), then the pitch drawn like the PNG (no ghost, selection or handles, the route key under it if the Legend layer is on), then the phase's note as a caption in large text, easy to read on a phone: each line at least 4% of the frame's height, wrapped to at most 3 lines and cut with "…" if longer. The caption changes with the phase. The frame keeps one size through the play: the caption has room for as many lines as the longest note needs, and none if no phase has a note. Present mode's caption isn't changed by this. Badges go as their routes start moving, as when playing. The last frame is held 1.5 seconds, before the GIF starts again or the video ends.
    - **GIF**: 12 frames a second, with one palette of up to 255 colours shared by every frame (made from 8 frames spread through the play). After the first frame, pixels that haven't changed are stored as transparent, so each frame only holds what moved and the file stays small. It's encoded with [gifenc](https://github.com/mattdesl/gifenc) **1.0.3** (MIT licence), kept in the repo as `vendor/gifenc.esm.js` (the package's `dist/gifenc.esm.js`, without its source map line) with its licence in `vendor/gifenc.LICENSE`. It is only loaded the first time a GIF is made. To update it, replace both files from the new npm package's `dist/gifenc.esm.js` and `LICENSE.md`, and change the version here.
    - **Video**: 30 frames a second, at 8 Mbps (more for a picture over 1080 px wide, in proportion), so it's as sharp as the app, recorded from a canvas with `MediaRecorder`: `video/mp4` where the browser can record it, otherwise WebM. The recorder is paused while each frame is drawn and runs for one frame's time once it's ready, so the video keeps the play's timing however long the drawing takes. Making a video takes at least as long as the video lasts.

    **A position's animation**: with a position picked in **Who**, the animation follows that player as the phone viewer does, made for sending to them: an upright pitch (attack playing up), framed on the player and gliding after them (`js/follow.js`, the viewer's own camera), their team-mates faded and far opponents half faded, a yellow ring on them. The frame is phone shaped, whatever the Size: GIF 720 × 1280, video 1080 × 1920 (the Size choice is greyed out, and a tip says so). It has a header (the team logo if it's shown on exports, the play name, *Phase n of N · Attack 12*), the pitch, and at the bottom *YOUR JOB* with the player's job for the phase (written, or made from their routes) in large bold text, shrunk to fit two lines. Their cues pop up next to them in a yellow bubble, as in the viewer, timed as the viewer plays it: each at its step, held 1.5 s, with playback easing to half speed for about a second. The route key isn't drawn. The file is named `<play name> - <team> <number>.gif` (or `.mp4` / `.webm`), e.g. `Blind side - Attack 12.gif`.

    The page stays responsive while it works: it waits for each frame's picture to load before the next.

  Every save and export shows a short message in the status line, e.g. *Saved Blind side.pdf*. A PDF says *Making the PDF…* while it's being made. Each picture is made from a copy of the play taken when you ask for it, and editing is locked (as while playing) until it's ready, so a change can't end up half in it.

  Loading a play (from a file, a backup or the library) also repairs it: a player missing from a phase is given their spot from the nearest earlier phase, or the nearest later one if there is no earlier one. Older versions could leave a player out of some phases, e.g. one deleted and added back while on phase 1.

## Versioned files and updates

Browsers keep old copies of JS and CSS files, so right after a deploy one could load the new `view.js` against a cached old `model.js` and stop with *does not provide an export named …*. So every file is loaded with a version: `index.html` and `view.html` load `styles.css` / `view.css` and their module script as `…?v=<version>`, and an import map in each sends every local module (`js/*.js` and `vendor/gifenc.esm.js`) to the same file with `?v=<version>`. Lazy loads (jsPDF, gifenc) take the version from their own module's address. Old and new files never mix: a new version is a new set of addresses.

The version is a short hash of every site JS and CSS file. **Run `npm run stamp` before each commit that changes JS or CSS**: it writes the hash into both pages (and rebuilds the import map from the files in `js/` and `vendor/`, so a new module is covered). It needs nothing but Node, and the site still has no build step: the pages are committed stamped. `npm test` fails if the stamp doesn't match the files (`tests/stamp.test.js`).

If a page still can't start (the app hasn't drawn, or the viewer hasn't opened the play, within 8 seconds, or a module fails to load), a small inline script in each page replaces it with *Couldn't load the app. This can happen right after an update.* and a **Reload** button. Reload loads the page again with a fresh `?r=<time>`, so the browser fetches the new page and its new versions, and keeps the `#hash`, so a share link still opens its play (and `#phase=` its phase). In the viewer, any uncaught error while *Opening the play…* is up shows the same message. The app tells that script it's ready with `window.appReady()`.

## Deploying to GitHub Pages

1. Run `npm run stamp` if JS or CSS changed (see *Versioned files and updates*), commit, and push the repo to GitHub.
2. Go to **Settings → Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to *Deploy from a branch*, then pick your branch (e.g. `main`) and the `/ (root)` folder. Save.
4. After about a minute the site will be live at `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`.

All paths are relative, so the site also works from a project sub-path. The empty `.nojekyll` file turns off Jekyll, so `llms.txt` and `docs/AI_PLAY_GUIDE.md` are served exactly as they are in the repo (as plain text and Markdown), not turned into pages.

## Coordinates and data

Everything is in metres. The SVG `viewBox` is in metres too.

- `x`: 0–100 between the try lines. In-goal runs from −10 to 0 and from 100 to 110.
- `y`: 0–70 from touchline to touchline.

The whole play is a single state object, exposed as `window.playState` for debugging:

```json
{
  "schemaVersion": 1,
  "name": "Untitled play",
  "category": "attack",
  "tags": ["backs", "set move"],
  "view": "full",
  "orientation": "horizontal",
  "players": [{ "id": "a10", "team": "attack", "label": "10" }],
  "phases": [{
    "note": "",
    "positions": { "a10": { "x": 58, "y": 25 } },
    "objects": [
      { "id": "o1", "type": "ball", "x": 60, "y": 25 },
      { "id": "o2", "type": "scrum", "x": 40, "y": 15, "putIn": "attack", "pack": ["a1", "a2", "…", "d8"] }
    ],
    "routes": [
      { "id": "r1", "type": "run", "from": "a10", "points": [[58, 25], [66, 29]], "curve": 0, "order": 1 },
      { "id": "r2", "type": "pass", "from": "a10", "to": "a12", "points": [[58, 25], [60, 35]], "curve": 0, "order": 2 }
    ]
  }]
}
```

- Player ids are `a1`–`a15` (attack) and `d1`–`d15` (defense). `label` is what gets drawn.
- A route's `from` is a player id, or `null` if it was drawn from open grass. A pass also has `to`: the receiver's id, or `null` for a loose end. Its last point sits on the receiver. Loading a play sets a `from` or `to` that names a player who isn't in the play to `null`.
- `curve` is how far, in metres, the middle of the route is pushed sideways from the straight line between its first and last point. Positive bends to the right of the direction of travel, as seen on screen. The route is drawn as a quadratic Bézier.
- A pass or kick that is one of a player's options has `option`: `"A"`, `"B"`, `"C"` …, and may have `optionLabel`, a string of up to 25 characters. Other routes have `option: null` (or no `option`, in older plays). A player's options in a phase share one `order`.
- A route's `order` is a whole number from 1 to 99 and sets the order routes run in. A new pass or kick gets one more than the highest existing order; a new run, decoy, drift or blitz gets the highest (1 if there's none). Numbers can repeat, and gaps are fine. Routes without `order` (older data) simply show no badge; **Order** in the route bar gives one the next number.
- Loading checks every optional field, and drops it (or uses its default) if it's the wrong type: `option` (a single capital letter, passes and kicks only), `optionLabel` (text, cut to 25 characters), `order` (a whole number of 1 or more), `curve` (a finite number, else 0), `pack` (a list of ids of players in the play), `putIn` and `throwIn` (`"attack"` or `"defense"`), `tags` (text only), `name` (cut to 60 characters), `note` (text) and `instructions` (text for players in the play, trimmed, cut to 120 characters; empty ones dropped, and the field left out when none are left). Positions of players who aren't in the play are dropped, and missing or broken ones are filled in.
- `orientation` is `"horizontal"` or `"vertical"`. It only changes how the pitch is drawn: all coordinates stay in the same pitch metres. Vertical is a quarter turn anticlockwise, so pitch (x, y) is drawn at SVG (y, −x).
- A lineout made with the preset has `throwIn` (`"attack"` or `"defense"`) and `pack`, the ids of the players that move with it; its `y` is always 5 or 65. A plain `lineout` marker without `pack` is just a marker.
- A scrum made with the preset has `putIn` (`"attack"` or `"defense"`) and `pack`, the ids of the players that move with it. Its `x, y` is the mark between the front rows, with `y` always 15, 35 or 55. A plain `scrum` object without `pack` (older data) still draws as an "S" marker.
- `category` is one of `attack`, `defense`, `lineout` and `scrum`. `tags` is a list of strings.
- `phases` is in play order. Each phase has its own `note`, player `positions`, `objects` (markers) and `routes`, and optionally `instructions`: each player's job in that phase, by player id (`{ "a10": "Flat, call for it early." }`), and `cues`: `[{ "player": "a13", "step": 2, "text": "JUMP!" }]`, both shown by the phone viewer and a position's animation. Loading checks cues: the player must be in the play, the text is trimmed and cut to 30 characters (empty ones dropped), and `step` must be a whole number from 1 to 99, else it's the order of the player's first route in the phase, or 1. The field is left out when no cues are left. Plays saved before it existed load as they are. Players are listed once, in `players`, and every player has a position in every phase (loading fills in any that are missing).
- The team logo and team name are a setting of the browser (see *Team logo*), never part of a play.
- Which phase is being shown (also in the address as `#phase=n`), the layer checkboxes (Step numbers and Player size are remembered by the browser), the play speed, Loop and chosen option, Present mode and its pen drawings, and the undo history are view settings and aren't part of the play. The library stores the shown phase alongside the play. `orientation` is saved with the play but is a view choice too: turning the pitch is not an undo step.
- `schemaVersion` is still 1: `tags` and the newer fields are optional, and older plays load as they are.
